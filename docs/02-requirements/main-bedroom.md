## Requirements from 2026-10-31 (Design 5 onwards)
- **King size bed** (SG king 1830 × 1910 mattress). Bed base no bigger than the mattress (divan / platform), max **1850 × 1930 + 80 headboard**, or the clearances below don't hold.
- **No mirror directly facing the bed** (applies to the vanity mirror and the full-length mirror).
- **Vanity 600 W × 600 D**, or none if space doesn't allow.
- **Wardrobe at least 3000 long in total**; may be L-shaped. Still the wall-mounted metal system (rules below).
- Wardrobe split **His / Hers by zone**: one person gets the north half (double hang + 2 baskets), the other the south half (2 baskets + long hang).
- **Blackout curtains** on the window, with a 150 gap behind the headboard so they hang freely.
- Trade-off accepted: a king bed, a 3 m wardrobe and the study table don't all fit with an aisle on both sides of the bed, so the bed goes against the west wall and the west sleeper gets in from the foot.

## Wardrobe rules (all designs from now on)
- **No carpentry for the wardrobe.** Always a wall-mounted metal system: slotted **wall standards + brackets**, **poles** to hang clothes, wire shelves, and **mesh baskets mounted on the brackets that slide out** (like the owner's reference videos).
- **Doors: always bi-fold doors that fold and slide to the sides** (free-sliding on one top track).

### Feng shui check — foot of the bed vs the WC1 door (2026-10-31)
- The foot of the bed faces south. The WC1 bi-fold (x 11950–12650) is in line with the **west half of the foot**, ~2.1 m away. Traditional feng shui calls a bed whose foot faces a toilet door unfavourable; the WC isn't behind the headboard, which is the other common fault.
- What softens it here: the **study table sits between** the west sleeper's feet and the door; the toilet bowl inside WC1 is at the west end (x ~11235), **not visible through the doorway** from the bed; the bed is **not in line with the room door**; the headboard is on a solid wall (the window wall, with a 900 headboard + curtains).
- Remedies, cheapest first: keep the WC bi-fold and toilet lid closed (also keeps humidity and smells out); a ceiling-hung curtain or a tall plant / screen near the WC door; a full-height door that matches the wall. The only layout that turns the foot away is the Design 4 orientation (headboard west, foot east), which brings back the bed under the window and the tight foot-of-bed gap.

### Gap between the bed and the wardrobe: 535 mm
- Enough to walk through, **too tight for swing doors** (a 450–500 leaf would hit the bed) or for drawers. Bi-folds stick out ~250 when open — also tight. **Use sliding doors** (or a curtain) for the bays facing the bed.
- Comfortable is 600 minimum, 750+ to dress in. Ways to gain space: wardrobe 550 deep (+50), a slim wall-mounted headboard instead of an 80 mm one (+~60) → ~645.
- **Check the bed frame length before buying**: many king frames are 1950–2100 incl. the headboard; a 2100 frame leaves only ~425.
- The bed only blocks the first ~1.8 m of the wardrobe; drawers sit in the bays past it.

### Open window end — is it viable?
Yes, with care:
- **Good:** daylight and air help a lot against mould and musty smells in Singapore's humidity.
- **Sun fades clothes** (dark colours, silk, denim) near the window. Use UV-blocking window film, or keep that bay for things that don't fade (luggage, bags in dust covers, boxes).
- Only ~325 mm of the window (x 14425–14750) is in front of the wardrobe, so the light mainly reaches the first bay; the rest stays dim. Direct sun depends on which way the window really faces.
- That strip of glass ends up inside the wardrobe: check that no window handle or opening sash is trapped behind it, keep enough room to open and clean it, and don't fix carpentry to the window frame (HDB).
- Clothes are visible from outside and get rain/dust if the window is left open. The curtain (or a separate roller blind) needs to cover that strip too.
